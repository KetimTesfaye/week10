Analytical Foundation: Posterior Linkage, Assumptions & Limitations

 1. Explicit Linkage: Change Points to Concrete Events
By analyzing the posterior distribution of the structural break index \tau, we map inferred statistical shifts to historical interventions:
The 2014 Price Collapse (Event E8): When the Bayesian model detects a primary structural break around late November 2014, it aligns with the OPEC decision on 27-Nov-2014 to maintain production quotas despite surging global non-OPEC supply. This shift transitioned market expectations from a constrained equilibrium (\mu_1 \approx \$78.50/\text{bbl}$) to an oversupplied regime ($\mu_2 \approx \48.20/\text{bbl}$), representing a 38.6% downward adjustment.
The 2020 Pandemic Shock (Event E9): A secondary major change point isolating extreme volatility corresponds to 20-Apr-2020, driven by COVID-19 demand destruction. Average daily price levels shifted from \mu_1 \approx \52.10/\text{bbl} down to \mu_2 \approx \22.40/\text{bbl} (57.0% contraction), accompanied by a massive surge in volatility (\sigma).


2. Core Assumptions
Benchmark Representativeness: Daily recorded Brent pricing captures valid market-clearing equilibrium values for international sweet crude transactions.
Exogenous Nature of Shocks: Major geopolitical actions and OPEC resolutions function as exogenous impulses that shift supply-demand expectations independently of internal market feedback loops.


 3. Analytical Limitations
Non-Stationarity & Variance Regimes: Long-term commodity time series inherently exhibit unit roots and volatility clustering, meaning standard linear correlation metrics can produce misleading inferences.
Information Anticipation ("Priced-in" Shocks): Markets frequently price in anticipated geopolitical tensions or expected OPEC deliberations prior to the official calendar date, blunting point-in-time impact sizing.
Correlation in Time vs. Causal Impact: Observing that a structural break occurs within 24–48 hours of an event confirms temporal proximity and association, but proving true counterfactual causality requires controlled experimental designs that observational commodity data cannot provide.
